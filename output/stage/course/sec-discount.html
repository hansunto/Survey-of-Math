    Section A.3 Using percentages:tax, discount amount

    Definition 8.16. Sales Tax.

    A common application of percent involves sales tax collected by states, counties, and cities on sales of items to customers.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sales tax amount} = \text{tax rate} \times \text{item's cost} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.17.

    Suppose that the local sales tax rate is \(7.5\%\) and you purchase a bicycle for \(\$394\text{.}\) How much tax is paid and what is the bicycle’s total cost?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the sales tax amount by converting the percent to a decimal (\(7.5\% = 0.075\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Tax} = 0.075 \times \$394 = \$29.55 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Add the tax amount to the original price to find the total cost:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Cost} = \$394 + \$29.55 = \$423.55 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.18.

    Suppose your lunch bill is \(\$9.84\text{.}\) Approximately how much would a \(15\%\) tip be? A \(20\%\) tip?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Convert percentages to decimals to find the approximate values:
    🔗
    • 15% Tip:
      \begin{equation*} 0.15 \times \$9.84 \approx \$1.48 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    • 20% Tip:
      \begin{equation*} 0.20 \times \$9.84 \approx \$1.97 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.19.

    Find the total price of a new 32-inch OLED TV that sells for \(\$219.99\) under the following scenarios:
    🔗
    1. If purchased in Georgia, where the sales tax is \(4\%\text{.}\)
      🔗
    2. If purchased in Kentucky, where the sales tax is \(6\%\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    • Georgia (4%):
      \begin{equation*} \text{Tax} = 0.04 \times \$219.99 \approx \$8.80 \end{equation*}
      \begin{equation*} \text{Total} = \$219.99 + \$8.80 = \$228.79 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    • Kentucky (6%):
      \begin{equation*} \text{Tax} = 0.06 \times \$219.99 \approx \$13.20 \end{equation*}
      \begin{equation*} \text{Total} = \$219.99 + \$13.20 = \$233.19 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 8.20. Discount.

    Discount is another application of percent. The discount rate is a percent of the original price, implying the following formula:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discount amount} = \text{discount rate} \times \text{original price} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.21. Percents and sale price.

    A computer with an original price of \(\$1,460\) is on sale at \(15\%\) off. What is the discount amount and what is the computer’s sale price?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the discount amount:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discount} = 0.15 \times \$1,460 = \$219.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Subtract the discount from the original price to find the sale price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sale Price} = \$1,460 - \$219.00 = \$1,241.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.22.

    An online company is advertising a Presto Pizza Oven on sale for \(45\%\) off the original price of \(\$69.99\text{.}\) What is the sale price for the oven?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the discount amount:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discount} = 0.45 \times \$69.99 \approx \$31.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Calculate the sale price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sale Price} = \$69.99 - \$31.50 = \$38.49 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Alternative method: Pay \(55\%\) (\(100\% - 45\%\)) of the original price:
    \begin{equation*} 0.55 \times \$69.99 \approx \$38.49 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.23.

    A shirt is on sale for \(40\%\) off and you have an additional \(20\%\) off coupon. True or False: The shirt is now \(60\%\) off the original price.
    🔗
    Solution.
    False. Discounts are applied sequentially, not added together.
    🔗
    If a shirt costs \(\$100\text{,}\) a \(40\%\) discount drops it to \(\$60\text{.}\) The additional \(20\%\) coupon is then applied to the sale price of \(\$60\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Coupon savings} = 0.20 \times \$60 = \$12 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Final Price} = \$60 - \$12 = \$48 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Since you pay \(\$48\) on a \(\$100\) shirt, the actual total discount is \(52\%\text{,}\) not \(60\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Suppose a local sales tax rate is \(4.5\%\) and you purchase a car for \(\$28,500\text{.}\) How much sales tax is paid? How much is the total cost of the car?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the sales tax paid:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Tax} = 0.045 \times \$28,500 = \$1,282.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Calculate the total cost:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Cost} = \$28,500 + \$1,282.50 = \$29,782.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A local store is having a sale. All waterproof shoes are \(40\%\) off. If a pair of rain boots normally costs \(\$13.72\) plus \(7\%\) sales tax, how much will it cost to buy the rain boots during the sale? (Discounts are applied before sales tax is added.)
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Apply the \(40\%\) discount first to find the sale price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discount Amount} = 0.40 \times \$13.72 \approx \$5.49 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sale Price} = \$13.72 - \$5.49 = \$8.23 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Calculate the \(7\%\) sales tax on the discounted price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sales Tax} = 0.07 \times \$8.23 \approx \$0.58 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Find the final total cost:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Cost} = \$8.23 + \$0.58 = \$8.81 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    You went shopping for clothes during a sale that states everything is \(60\%\) off original prices. The original prices are: \(\$55.00\) for each pair of pants, \(\$35.00\) for each shirt, and \(\$49.00\) for each sweater. You want 2 pairs of pants, 3 shirts, and one sweater. What is the discounted amount? How much do you pay for the clothes with a \(6.25\%\) sales tax?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the total subtotal at original retail prices:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Pants: } 2 \times \$55.00 = \$110.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Shirts: } 3 \times \$35.00 = \$105.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sweater: } 1 \times \$49.00 = \$49.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Original Total} = \$110.00 + \$105.00 + \$49.00 = \$264.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Calculate the total discounted amount saved (\(60\%\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discounted Amount} = 0.60 \times \$264.00 = \$158.40 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Determine the actual clothing sale price before tax:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sale Price} = \$264.00 - \$158.40 = \$105.60 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    4. Add the \(6.25\%\) sales tax to the sale price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sales Tax} = 0.0625 \times \$105.60 = \$6.60 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Paid} = \$105.60 + \$6.60 = \$112.20 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Suppose you go to a restaurant and want to give your waitress a \(15\%\) tip. The check total was \(\$65.00\text{.}\) What is the tip? How much would the total come to?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the tip amount:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Tip} = 0.15 \times \$65.00 = \$9.75 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Compute the final total bill:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Bill} = \$65.00 + \$9.75 = \$74.75 \end{equation*}
    🔗
