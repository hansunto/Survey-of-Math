    Section A.4 Percent decrease, Percent increase

    Definition 8.24. Percent Increase and Decrease.

    Percents are used for comparing changes such as increases or decreases in sales, population, prices, and production. If a quantity changes, its percent increase or its percent decrease can be formed as:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\text{amount of increase}}{\text{original amount}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{amount of decrease}}{\text{original amount}} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Find the percent increase (or decrease) and then express the fraction as a percent.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.25. Percent decrease.

    A jacket regularly sells for \(\$135.00\text{.}\) The sale price is \(\$60.75\text{.}\) Find the percent decrease of the sale price from the regular price.
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the amount of decrease by subtracting the sale price from the regular price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amount of decrease} = \$135.00 - \$60.75 = \$74.25 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Divide the decrease by the original regular price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\$74.25}{\$135.00} = 0.55 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Convert the decimal to a percentage:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0.55 \times 100 = 55\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The sale price represents a 55% decrease from the regular price.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.26. Percents of percents.

    John Tesh, while he was still co-anchoring “Entertainment Tonight,” reported that the PBS series “The Civil War” had an audience of \(13\%\) versus the usual \(4\%\) PBS audience, “an increase of more than 300%.” Did Tesh report the percent increase correctly?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the raw amount of increase in the audience percentage points:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amount of increase} = 13\% - 4\% = 9\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Divide this increase by the original baseline audience share (\(4\%\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{9}{4} = 2.25 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Convert this relative increase into a percentage:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 2.25 \times 100 = 225\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    No, Tesh did not report it correctly. The true percent increase was 225%. While \(13\%\) is more than 3 times (300% of) the original \(4\%\text{,}\) the relative change/increase itself is only 225%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.27.

    Find the percent change if 14 is increased to 70.
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the amount of increase:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amount of increase} = 70 - 14 = 56 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Divide the increase by the original value (14):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{56}{14} = 4 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Express the result as a percentage:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4 \times 100 = 400\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The percent change is a 400% increase.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.28.

    A television regularly sells for \(\$940\text{.}\) The sale price is \(\$611\text{.}\) Find the percent decrease of the sale price from the regular price.
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Calculate the amount of decrease:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amount of decrease} = \$940 - \$611 = \$329 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Divide by the original regular price:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\$329}{\$940} = 0.35 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Convert to a percentage:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0.35 \times 100 = 35\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The television has a 35% decrease in price.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 8.29.

    If a laptop computer regularly costs \(\$879\) and is on sale for \(\$599\text{,}\) what percentage discount is being given? Round to the nearest hundredth if needed.
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the total markdown discount amount:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Discount amount} = \$879 - \$599 = \$280 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Divide the markdown by the original baseline cost:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\$280}{\$879} \approx 0.318544 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Convert to a percentage and round to the nearest hundredth:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0.318544 \times 100 \approx 31.85\% \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The percentage discount being given is approximately 31.85%.
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Determine whether each statement makes sense or not, and explain your reasoning.
    🔗
    1. I have \(\$100\) and my restaurant bill comes to \(\$80\text{,}\) which is not enough to leave a \(20\%\) tip.
      🔗
    2. I found the percent decrease in a jacket’s price to be \(120\%\text{.}\)
      🔗
    3. My rent increased from \(20\%\) to \(30\%\) of my income, so the percent increase is \(10\%\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    • (a) Does not make sense. A \(20\%\) tip on an \(\$80\) bill is calculated as:
      \begin{equation*} 0.20 \times \$80 = \$16 \end{equation*}
      The total cost of the meal with the tip added is:
      \begin{equation*} \$80 + \$16 = \$96 \end{equation*}
      Since \(\$96 \lt \$100\text{,}\) you have more than enough money to cover it.
      🔗
    • (b) Does not make sense. A percent decrease represents how much value an item loses relative to its starting point. A \(100\%\) decrease means the item is completely free (\(\$0\)). A decrease of \(120\%\) would mean the retail store is paying you money to take the jacket away, which is impossible in standard retail settings.
      🔗
    • (c) Does not make sense. The raw difference between the values is \(10\) percentage points, but the percent increase must be calculated relative to the original baseline amount (\(20\%\)):
      \begin{equation*} \text{Percent Increase} = \frac{\text{Change}}{\text{Original}} = \frac{30 - 20}{20} = \frac{10}{20} = 0.50 \rightarrow 50\% \end{equation*}
      Your rent contribution actually experienced a 50% increase.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Suppose a local sales tax rate is \(4.5\%\) and you purchase a car for \(\$28,500\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. How much sales tax is paid?
      🔗
    2. How much is the total cost of the car?
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the sales tax paid by converting the rate to a decimal (\(4.5\% = 0.045\)):
      \begin{equation*} \text{Sales Tax} = 0.045 \times \$28,500 = \$1,282.50 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    2. Add the sales tax to the base purchase price to find the total final invoice cost:
      \begin{equation*} \text{Total Cost} = \$28,500 + \$1,282.50 = \$29,782.50 \end{equation*}
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Suppose that you drive 15,000 miles per year and gas averages \(\$3.99\) per gallon. What will you save in annual fuel expenses by owning a hybrid car averaging 45 miles per gallon rather than an SUV averaging 28 miles per gallon?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Calculate the total annual fuel costs for the SUV:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Gallons used} = \frac{15,000 \text{ miles}}{28 \text{ mpg}} \approx 535.71 \text{ gallons} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{SUV Fuel Cost} = 535.71 \text{ gallons} \times \$3.99 \approx \$2,137.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Calculate the total annual fuel costs for the hybrid car:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Gallons used} = \frac{15,000 \text{ miles}}{45 \text{ mpg}} = 333.33 \text{ gallons} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Hybrid Fuel Cost} = 333.33 \text{ gallons} \times \$3.99 = \$1,330.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    3. Subtract to find the net financial savings:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Annual Savings} = \$2,137.50 - \$1,330.00 = \$807.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    You can expect to save \(\$807.50\) every year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    If you are selling your house with a local Realtor who requires a \(6\%\) commission fee, what can you expect to pay the Realtor if your house sells for \(\$339,000\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Convert the percentage commission fee to a decimal multiplier (\(6\% = 0.06\)) and multiply by the total closing sale valuation:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Commission Fee} = 0.06 \times \$339,000 = \$20,340.00 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    You can expect to pay the agent a fee of \(\$20,340.00\).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A new wireless printer costs \(\$99.99\) in the store. What would your total cost be if sales tax is \(9.5\%\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Find the total isolated sales tax surcharged (\(9.5\% = 0.095\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Sales Tax} = 0.095 \times \$99.99 \approx \$9.50 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Add the tax onto the shelf value to find the out-of-pocket register payment:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Total Cost} = \$99.99 + \$9.50 = \$109.49 \end{equation*}
    🔗
