    1.

    Find the mean, median, and mode for the following dataset:
    \begin{equation*} \{29, 24, 31, 40, 30, 35, 25, 28\} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    To simplify the statistical calculations, we first arrange the dataset of \(n = 8\) values in ascending order:
    \begin{equation*} \{24, 25, 28, 29, 30, 31, 35, 40\} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    1. Mean Calculation: The mean is calculated by summing all the values and dividing by the total number of data points (\(n = 8\)):
    \begin{align*} \text{Mean} \amp = \frac{24 + 25 + 28 + 29 + 30 + 31 + 35 + 40}{8}\\ \text{Mean} \amp = \frac{242}{8} = 30.25 \end{align*}
    Thus, the mean of the dataset is 30.25.
    🔗
    2. Median Calculation: Since there is an even number of data points (\(8\)), the median is found by taking the average of the two middle values (the 4th and 5th numbers in our ordered list, which are \(29\) and \(30\)):
    \begin{equation*} \text{Median} = \frac{29 + 30}{2} = 29.5 \end{equation*}
    Thus, the median of the dataset is 29.5.
    🔗
    3. Mode Calculation: The mode is the value that appears most frequently in a dataset. Because every unique number in this list occurs exactly once, there is no mode.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    The following temperatures were recorded (in \(^\circ\)F) each day for two weeks:
    \begin{equation*} \{82, 72, 83, 75, 80, 78, 82, 73, 60, 79, 80, 78, 83, 81\} \end{equation*}
    1. What is the outlier in this data, if one exists?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. What is the mean for this set of data?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What is the mean for this set of data, if the outlier is removed?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    To perform our analysis, we first arrange the \(n = 14\) recorded data entries in ascending order:
    \begin{equation*} \{60, 72, 73, 75, 78, 78, 79, 80, 80, 81, 82, 82, 83, 83\} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Part a) Outlier Evaluation: Using the standard Interquartile Range (\(\text{IQR}\)) method, we identify the middle parameters of our distributions:
    • First Quartile (\(Q_1\)) at position 4 is \(75\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Third Quartile (\(Q_3\)) at position 11 is \(82\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 82 - 75 = 7\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    The lower cutoff threshold for anomalies is defined by:
    \begin{equation*} \text{Lower Bound} = Q_1 - 1.5(\text{IQR}) = 75 - 1.5(7) = 64.5 \end{equation*}
    Because the data value \(60\) falls below the \(64.5\) bound, 60\(^\circ\) is a confirmed outlier.
    🔗
    Part b) Original Mean Calculation: Summing all 14 data entries gives a total value of \(1086\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{Mean} = \frac{1086}{14} \approx 77.57^\circ\text{F} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Part c) Cleaned Mean Calculation: Subtracting the outlier score of 60 leaves \(n = 13\) entries with a total combined value of \(1026\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{Cleaned Mean} = \frac{1026}{13} \approx 78.92^\circ\text{F} \end{equation*}
    Removing the low outlier slightly shifts the average higher by roughly \(1.35^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The rainfall in a small town is recorded daily for two weeks, in inches:
    \begin{equation*} \{1.2, 2.3, 0.8, 2.3, 1.0, 3.1, 4.2, 1.4, 2.1, 0.4, 1.3, 2.0, 1.8, 3.2\} \end{equation*}
    What are the mean and the sample standard deviation for this data set, rounded to the nearest hundredths?
    🔗
    Solution.
    To compute our summary metrics, we analyze the complete data pool containing \(n = 14\) entries.
    🔗
    Step 1: Calculate the Mean (\(\bar{x}\)): Summing all 14 precipitation values yields a total of \(27.1\) inches:
    \begin{equation*} \bar{x} = \frac{27.1}{14} \approx 1.94 \end{equation*}
    The mean rainfall over the two-week period is 1.94 inches.
    🔗
    Step 2: Calculate the Sample Standard Deviation (\(s\)): Using the sample standard deviation expression with a denominator of \(n - 1 = 13\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Summing the squared deviances from the actual calculated mean results in an accumulated value of approximately \(14.1521\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} s = \sqrt{\frac{14.1521}{13}} = \sqrt{1.0886} \approx 1.04 \end{equation*}
    Thus, the sample standard deviation is 1.04 inches.
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    The heights of adult men in a certain city are normally distributed with a mean (\(\mu\)) of 70 inches and a standard deviation (\(\text{SD}\)) of 3 inches. Use the Empirical Rule to answer the following questions:
    1. What percentage of men are between 67 and 73 inches tall?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. What percentage of men are between 61 and 79 inches tall?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What percentage of men are taller than 76 inches?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The Empirical Rule (also known as the 68-95-99.7 rule) states that for a perfectly normal distribution:
    • Approximately \(68\%\) of the data falls within \(1\) standard deviation of the mean (\(\mu \pm 1\text{SD}\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Approximately \(95\%\) of the data falls within \(2\) standard deviations of the mean (\(\mu \pm 2\text{SD}\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Approximately \(99.7\%\) of the data falls within \(3\) standard deviations of the mean (\(\mu \pm 3\text{SD}\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Given parameters: Mean \(\mu = 70\) inches and Standard Deviation \(\text{SD} = 3\) inches. Let us map out the standard deviation markers:
    • \(\displaystyle \mu \pm 1\text{SD} = 70 \pm 3 = [67, 73]\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \mu \pm 2\text{SD} = 70 \pm 6 = [64, 76]\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \mu \pm 3\text{SD} = 70 \pm 9 = [61, 79]\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Part a) Interval [67, 73]: These values correspond exactly to \(\mu - 1\text{SD}\) and \(\mu + 1\text{SD}\text{.}\) According to the rule, approximately 68% of the men fall within this height range.
    🔗
    Part b) Interval [61, 79]: These values correspond exactly to \(\mu - 3\text{SD}\) and \(\mu + 3\text{SD}\text{.}\) According to the rule, approximately 99.7% of the men fall within this height range.
    🔗
    Part c) Heights above 76 inches: A height of 76 inches corresponds exactly to \(\mu + 2\text{SD}\text{.}\) Since a normal curve is perfectly symmetrical, \(50\%\) of the data falls above the mean of 70. The region between the mean (70) and 2 standard deviations above the mean (76) contains exactly half of the \(95\%\) range, which is \(47.5\%\text{.}\) To find the tail value remaining above 76:
    \begin{equation*} 50\% - 47.5\% = 2.5\% \end{equation*}
    Thus, approximately 2.5% of men in the city are taller than 76 inches.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    What is the z-score of the price of a pair of skis that cost $247, if the mean ski price is $279, with a standard deviation of $16?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given parameters: value \(x = 247\text{,}\) mean \(\mu = 279\text{,}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 16\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute these values into the z-score formula:
    \begin{equation*} z = \frac{247 - 279}{16} = \frac{-32}{16} = -2.0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The price of the skis is exactly 2.0 standard deviations below the mean.
    🔗

    What is the z-score of a 5-scoop ice cream cone if the mean number of scoops is 3, with a standard deviation of 1 scoop?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given parameters: value \(x = 5\text{,}\) mean \(\mu = 3\text{,}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute these values into the z-score formula:
    \begin{equation*} z = \frac{5 - 3}{1} = \frac{2}{1} = 2.0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The 5-scoop cone is exactly 2.0 standard deviations above the mean.

    7.

    What is the z-score of the weight of a cow that tips the scales at 825 lbs, if the mean weight for cows of her type is 1150 lbs, with a standard deviation of 77 lbs?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given parameters: value \(x = 825\text{,}\) mean \(\mu = 1150\text{,}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 77\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute these values into the z-score formula and round to two decimal places:
    \begin{equation*} z = \frac{825 - 1150}{77} = \frac{-325}{77} \approx -4.22 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The cow’s weight is approximately 4.22 standard deviations below the mean.

    8.

    What is the z-score of a measured value of 0.0034, given \(\mu = 0.0041\) and \(\sigma = 0.0008\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given parameters: value \(x = 0.0034\text{,}\) mean \(\mu = 0.0041\text{,}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 0.0008\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute these values into the standard z-score formula:
    \begin{equation*} z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{0.0034 - 0.0041}{0.0008} = \frac{-0.0007}{0.0008} = -0.875 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The measured value is exactly 0.875 standard deviations below the population mean.
    🔗
    🔗

    9.

    What is the z-score of a student’s test score of 89, if the class average was 80 with a standard deviation of 4 points?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given parameters: raw score \(x = 89\text{,}\) class mean \(\mu = 80\text{,}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute these values into the z-score expression:
    \begin{equation*} z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{89 - 80}{4} = \frac{9}{4} = 2.25 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The student’s test performance translates to a z-score of 2.25, meaning it is 2.25 standard deviations above the classroom average.

    10.

    A store is doing inventory for one week to determine the popularity of a new product. The table below shows the number of items sold each day. Find the linear regression equation that models this data, rounding coefficients to the nearest integer. Then, predict the number of items sold on the 10th day if the pattern of growth continues.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Calculate the Regression Equation Using the standard least-squares regression formulas with \(n = 7\) data entries, we calculate our foundational statistical summations:
    • \(\displaystyle \sum x = 28\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \sum y = 352\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \sum(x^2) = 140\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \sum(xy) = 1654\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Substituting these value totals into the slope and intercept expressions:
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{7(1654) - (28)(352)}{7(140) - (28)^2} = \frac{11578 - 9856}{980 - 784} = \frac{1722}{196} \approx 8.86 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} b = \frac{352 - (8.85714)(28)}{7} \approx 14.57 \end{equation*}
    Rounding both factors to the nearest integer values fields our linear modeling line equation:
    \begin{equation*} y = 9x + 14 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 2: Predict for Day 10 To estimate item performance on the tenth day, we evaluate our regression equation at \(x = 10\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} y \amp = 8.8571(10) + 14.5714\\ y \amp = 88.5714 + 14.5714 = 103.14 \end{align*}
    Rounding to the nearest whole item integer yields a final estimate of 104 items sold.
